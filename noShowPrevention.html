Design & Refinement Considerations
1
Minimum Training Volume
Predictive accuracy improves significantly beyond 500–1,000 historical records. Facilities with lower volume should use pooled FQHC benchmark data until sufficient local history exists.
2
Feature Weight Calibration
Weights in this demo are illustrative. A production model should derive weights from logistic regression or gradient boosting trained on facility-specific outcomes.
3
Equity & Disparity Monitoring
No-show risk correlates with SDOH factors tied to race and income. Regular bias audits must ensure the model supports — not penalizes — high-risk populations.
4
Closed-Loop Feedback
Retrain monthly using actual outcomes. Log outreach actions to measure which interventions reduce no-shows most effectively and update the playbook.
5
Outreach Tiering Protocol
Not all high-risk patients need the same intervention. Define a tiering protocol (call vs. SMS vs. CHW visit) based on risk score thresholds and available resources.
6
Appointment Type Sensitivity
Behavioral health, chronic disease management, and follow-up visits each carry different risk profiles and may warrant separate sub-models or adjustment factors.
7
Seasonal & Temporal Effects
No-show rates spike on Mondays, Fridays, and around holidays. Temporal features should be included as predictors and staff alerted during high-risk windows.
8
Provider Relationship Factor
Patients with an established PCP relationship show lower no-show rates. Panel assignment continuity and time-since-last-visit are meaningful signals.
9
Care Coordinator Workflow
Risk score alone is insufficient. Coordinators need actionable next steps, available resources, and an outreach logging mechanism — all at the point of care.
Demo Assumptions
All patient records are fully synthetic — generated to represent realistic FQHC population diversity. No real patient data is present.
Each patient has 4–7 synthetic visit history records with randomized but pattern-consistent no-show outcomes and reasons to simulate trend analysis.
Risk scores use a rule-based weighted algorithm across 7 factors. A production system would use an ML model trained on facility data.
Outreach questions are templated per no-show reason category — no API call required. In production, AI-generated questions per patient would be ideal.
The outreach tracker uses in-memory state only — data resets on page refresh. Production would persist to EHR or CRM.
Prevention outcome metrics (74% show rate with outreach) are illustrative benchmarks drawn from published FQHC no-show intervention literature.
